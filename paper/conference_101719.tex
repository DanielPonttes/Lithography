\begin{abstract}
This paper documents a source-only lithography pilot, separate from proposed joint source--mask optimization (SMO) and Neural-ILT work. The pilot optimizes 49 nonnegative pixelated illumination weights with unit total flux while holding seven FIT layouts fixed, using a scalar Abbe fixed-basis model and dose-weighted sigmoid resist. Three completed objective variants used five distinct feasible starts and completed their 204-step schedules, but none produced a checkpoint that passed the registered FIT gates. For schema 8, the new-layout mean was 161.333/35.667/93.333 pixels for PV-band, nominal $L_2$, and worst-dose $L_2$, compared with reference means 161.333/35.333/93.333. Calibration remained closed, and no final3 layout was indexed or evaluated. A subsequent fixed-grid diagnostic evaluated 5,140 source mixtures and found a best new-layout PV-band mean of 160.667 pixels, a 0.413\% decrease with unchanged mean nominal and worst-dose errors; one layout's nominal error nevertheless increased. These FIT results do not demonstrate process-window improvement, hotspot detection, generalization, or official-benchmark performance.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Optical proximity effects make it difficult to transfer a target layout to a wafer with low print error. Computational lithography studies models and optimization methods for this task, including source optimization and inverse lithography \cite{hopkins,neuralilt2}. Public benchmarks such as LithoBench support comparison across lithography and mask-optimization tasks \cite{lithobench}. Such prior work motivates joint approaches, but does not establish that a particular source-only experiment improves printability.

This paper reports a deliberately narrower result: a fixed-mask pilot that varies only the 49 weights of a pixelated illumination source. All seven FIT layouts remain fixed. No mask pixels or neural-network parameters are optimized, and the experiment does not train a hotspot classifier. An earlier October 7 attempt fell back to the same reference for all five seeds after its jittered starts failed the guard audit. The October 9 schema-6, schema-7, and schema-8 runs used distinct feasible starts, but none produced a checkpoint that met the qualification gate.

This pilot is motivated by earlier work in the broader project on Neural-ILT with PV-band heatmap fine-tuning. Joint SMO--Neural-ILT and heatmap-based hotspot analysis remain future work.

Robust pixelated source--mask optimization has previously been studied using a three-dimensional partially coherent imaging model \cite{robustsmo}. More recent bilevel work optimizes the coupled source and mask variables using an accelerated Abbe model \cite{bismo}. Those formulations provide context for the proposed extension; the present experiment holds masks and focus fixed and cannot inherit their reported quality gains.

\section{Background and Metrics}
\subsection{Forward Lithography and the Implemented Model}
Wave-optics models describe the formation of lithographic images under partially coherent illumination; Hopkins' diffraction theory is a foundational treatment \cite{hopkins}. The pilot described here uses a scalar Abbe implementation with a precomputed, fixed source basis. For a fixed mask and basis images $B_j$, its aerial intensity is linear in the source weights:
\begin{equation}
 I_{\mathbf{s}}(x,y)=\sum_{j=1}^{49}s_j B_j(x,y),\qquad
 s_j\geq 0,\qquad \sum_{j=1}^{49}s_j=1.
 \label{eq:source}
\end{equation}
This equation describes the implemented source parameterization; it is not a claim that the complete optical and resist pipeline is linear.

At dose $d$, the implementation applies a sigmoid resist response with steepness $\alpha=50$ and threshold $I_{\mathrm{th}}=0.225$, then thresholds the response at 0.5 to obtain a binary print $Z_{d,\mathbf{s}}$. The dose set is $\mathcal{D}=\{0.98,1.00,1.02\}$, and focus is fixed at zero.

\subsection{Hard PV-Band and Soft Objective}
For a layout target $T$, the reported hard PV-band area is the number of pixels printed at some but not all tested doses:
\begin{equation}
 A_{\mathrm{PV}} =
 \left|\left(\bigcup_{d\in\mathcal{D}}Z_{d,\mathbf{s}}\right)
 \setminus
 \left(\bigcap_{d\in\mathcal{D}}Z_{d,\mathbf{s}}\right)\right|.
 \label{eq:pv}
\end{equation}
Nominal $L_2$ is the number of binary mismatches to $T$ at dose 1.00. Worst-dose $L_2$ is the maximum mismatch count over the three doses. These are pixel counts on the evaluated raster.

Schema 6 uses the existing differentiable, target-aware critical-corner softcount averaged equally over all seven FIT layouts; schema 7 optimizes the same softcount averaged over only the three new FIT layouts. Schema 8 was a completed ablation minimizing a target-independent smooth dose-transition surrogate on the new three layouts with the same $\beta=200,400,800$ continuation schedule; its formula is given in Section~\ref{sec:optimization}. These soft objectives and their associated heatmaps are not the hard PV-band area in (\ref{eq:pv}), a labeled hotspot map, or hotspot-classification scores. In particular, no hotspot precision, recall, or F1 score is measured.

The physical continuous-resist heatmap at fixed focus is
\begin{equation}
 H_{50}(A)=\sigma\!\left(50(1.02A-0.225)\right)
          -\sigma\!\left(50(0.98A-0.225)\right),\quad A\geq0,
 \label{eq:physicalheatmap}
\end{equation}
where $A$ is aerial intensity and $\sigma$ is the logistic sigmoid. For $A\leq0.28$, the logistic-difference bound gives $H_{50}(A)\leq\tanh(A/2)\leq\tanh(0.14)<0.140$. For $A>0.28$, $H_{50}(A)\leq1-\sigma(2.47)=\sigma(-2.47)<0.079$. Thus $H_{50}(A)<0.15$ for all nonnegative aerial intensities under these pilot settings; a threshold above 0.15 on this heatmap cannot flag any pixel. This conditional bound concerns only the fixed-focus, $\alpha=50$ physical continuous-resist heatmap. It does not apply to the binary PV-band map, the target-aware critical softcount, the schema-8 optimizer's separate $\beta$-dependent smooth-PV objective, or a varying-focus process window.

\section{Implemented Pilot}
\subsection{Source and Layout Scope}
The pupil is discretized on a $9\times9$ grid in pupil coordinates $(u,v)$. The 49 permitted source points lie in the disk of normalized radius 0.9, including the center and grid points at radii below 0.3. The inner radius 0.3 defines the initial annular illumination profile; it does not restrict the source weights during optimization. The weights satisfy (\ref{eq:source}), so the modeled source has unit total flux. The frozen optical configuration uses NA 1.35, wavelength 193\,nm, a 4\,nm pixel pitch, and a $128\times128$ raster.

The source is the only optimized variable. Four original FIT layouts and three fixed synthetic FIT layouts form the seven-layout objective. Their masks and targets are held constant during optimization. Calibration data were excluded from optimization and selection; the final3 layouts were never indexed or evaluated.

\subsection{Optimization and Qualification}
\label{sec:optimization}
The registered solver is a Frank--Wolfe procedure with linear minimization over the guarded source-weight domain. Each seed has 68 steps at each of $\beta=200,400,800$, for 204 scheduled steps. Hard metrics are recorded at registered checkpoints. Schema 6 weights all seven layouts equally in its critical-softcount loss; schema 7 uses static equal weights over only the three new FIT layouts. Schema 8 used a direct smooth-PV surrogate (not the binary hard PV-band metric), with per-layout loss
\begin{equation}
 H_{\beta}(A)=\operatorname{mean}_{x,y}\left[\sigma\!\left(\beta(1.02A-0.225)\right)-\sigma\!\left(\beta(0.98A-0.225)\right)\right],
 \label{eq:optimizerpv}
\end{equation}
averaged equally over those same three layouts. Here $\beta$ is the optimizer continuation parameter, distinct from the physical resist steepness $\alpha=50$ in (\ref{eq:physicalheatmap}). Schema 8 has no target-fidelity penalty. The four original layouts remain in the unchanged guarded domain and hard qualification, and all variants retain the common all-seven critical-softcount mean at $\beta=800$ for checkpoint ranking. No variant uses dynamic or hotspot-dependent layout weights.

A checkpoint can qualify only if it preserves the original FIT limits (mean PV-band area at most 234.5 pixels, mean nominal $L_2$ equal to zero, and mean worst-dose $L_2$ at most 150.25 pixels), strictly improves the new-three mean PV-band relative to the fixed seed-17 reference, does not worsen the new-three nominal or worst-dose $L_2$ means, has no blank positive target at any tested dose, and passes the registered nominal-polytope and critical-guard audits. Calibration is opened only after all five seeds yield complete, qualified FIT vectors; it is not an optimizer input or tie-breaker.

\section{Experimental Setup}
The primary evidence is the frozen October 9 schema-6, schema-7, and schema-8 FIT reports, identified by full SHA-256 in the accompanying README. Each used seeds 17, 29, 43, 71, and 101 with distinct feasible starts and no fallback. Schema 6 completed 204 scheduled steps and four diagnostic snapshots per seed. Its minimum pairwise start distance was $L_1=0.374521$; each run had three accepted updates total and 201 stationary steps. All five schema-6 runs reached the same final FIT source.

The schema-6 report status is \texttt{no\_fit\_qualified\_checkpoint}; every seed had zero qualified checkpoints. Total runtime was 642.929195 seconds. The schema-7 report has the same failed status and zero qualified checkpoints; its five seeds completed 204 steps and four diagnostic snapshots, with one accepted update and 203 stationary iterations per seed. Total runtime was 586.786348 seconds. The schema-7 final vectors share one float32 hash, while their float64 values differ only at the last bits (maximum pairwise $L_1$ distance $2.6946\times10^{-14}$). Schema 8 also completed all 204 steps and eight diagnostic snapshots per seed (four critical-softcount and four smooth-PV); each seed had three accepted updates total and 201 stationary iterations. Its five distinct starts were the same feasible starting vectors as schema 6, with no fallback. All five schema-8 seeds reached a common float64 endpoint identity; runtime was 683.377778 seconds. Calibration remained closed for all three runs, and final3 layouts were never indexed or evaluated. The earlier October 7 schema-5 fallback attempt is historical context, not an independent replicate.

No optimized checkpoint passed the registered qualification gates. In schema 6, all five last checkpoints tied the reference PV-band and worst-dose $L_2$ means but had nominal $L_2$ mean 35.667 rather than 35.333, one additional aggregate nominal mismatch. In schema 7, the last new-three means were 162 PV-band pixels, 35 nominal mismatches, and 94.333 worst-dose mismatches. Relative to the reference, nominal $L_2$ improved by one aggregate mismatch, while aggregate PV-band count increased by two and worst-dose $L_2$ by three. The strict PV-band-improvement and no-worse nominal/worst-dose gates therefore failed. The five seeds within each run are computational restarts on the same fixed FIT layouts, not independent generalization evidence; a stationary Frank--Wolfe gap is not a certificate of global optimality. Calibration remained closed, and final3 layouts were never indexed or evaluated.

Schema 8 had the same new-three hard-metric means as schema 6: PV-band 161.333, nominal $L_2$ 35.667, and worst-dose $L_2$ 93.333, against reference means 161.333, 35.333, and 93.333. Thus it tied hard PV-band and worst-dose counts and had one additional aggregate nominal mismatch; the strict PV-band improvement and nominal non-regression requirements failed. Its common endpoint across five computational restarts is not independent evidence of generalization or global infeasibility.

For schema 6 only, the final-$\beta=800$ diagnostics show a same-beta surrogate tradeoff: from the phase boundary to the last FIT checkpoint, the original-four mean soft loss decreased by $7.372981\times10^{-6}$, while the new-three mean increased by $1.343352\times10^{-6}$. This is a measured change in that run's differentiable objective; it does not establish general incompatibility or explain hard metrics in schema 7. The reference-to-final hard-metric comparisons above use a different baseline and do not attribute changes to any single accepted step.


The completed source-segment diagnostic found 342 qualifying ordered slots, corresponding to 312 distinct float64 vectors and 282 distinct float32 vectors. The common FIT rank selected slot 4755 in seed group 101, on the reference-to-schema-7 endpoint segment at $\alpha=129/256$. Its original-four means remained 234.5/0/150.25 pixels, and its new-three means were 160.667/35.333/93.333. Thus aggregate new-three PV-band decreased from 484 to 482 pixels (0.413\%). This was a FIT-only diagnostic, not a new optimizer training run or independent test.

At the selected point, finite ribbons changed from 205 to 203 PV-band pixels and from 41 to 40 nominal mismatches; asymmetric line ends remained at 130 PV-band pixels but increased from 16 to 17 nominal mismatches. Irregular contacts remained at 149 PV-band pixels and 49 nominal mismatches. Worst-dose counts remained 118, 73, and 89, respectively. The mean-fidelity gate therefore passed despite a per-layout tradeoff. The seed-43 feasible initialization itself also qualified, with new-three PV-band 161 and unchanged mean fidelity; the earlier optimizer did not include its initial state as a selectable hard-metric checkpoint. These observations motivate preserving a hard-qualified incumbent from initialization onward. Reused terminal paths and shared FIT data prevent treating the five groups or hundreds of slots as independent replication.

\section{Limitations and Planned Validation}
\label{sec:limits}
This is a source-only computational pilot on seven fixed FIT layouts. Focus was held at zero while dose varied by $\pm2\%$; consequently, the run does not demonstrate a focus--dose process window. It does not evaluate mask changes, a Neural-ILT model, hotspot labels, hotspot-classification metrics, or an official LithoBench or MaskOpt test set. The differentiable softcount must not be described as a measured hard PV-band or hotspot-detection result.

No final3 result is claimed; those layouts were never indexed or evaluated.

The schema-6 feasible-start protocol and schema-7 static new-three critical-softcount ablation are implemented and benchmarked, but neither yielded a qualified checkpoint. Schema 8 is also implemented and benchmarked; its direct smooth-PV training objective did not yield a qualified checkpoint or improve the required hard FIT metrics. A direct hard-PV mixed-integer diagnostic is only a proposed read-only design; it has not been implemented or run. Any candidate would need a full float32 hard-gate audit; a global infeasibility claim would additionally require a certified bound that covers float32 threshold classification. The observed schema-8 failure does not prove that a qualifying source is infeasible. Future work will separately test joint source--mask optimization with a Neural-ILT model, define any hotspot target and evaluation metric, and conduct external evaluation on official LithoBench and MaskOpt data \cite{lithobench,maskopt}. Such evaluation requires a frozen protocol and independent test data; no generalization claim is made here.


\subsection{Registered Source-Segment Diagnostic}
A prospectively registered FIT-only diagnostic was implemented to examine intermediate source weights that the smooth optimizer checkpoints may have missed. For each of the five seeds, it registers four segments from the frozen reference to the schema-8 feasible start and the schema-6, schema-7, and schema-8 terminal vectors. Each segment uses $\alpha=k/256$, $k=0,\ldots,256$, giving 5,140 ordered slots. Each candidate is the unprojected float64 convex mixture $(1-\alpha)\mathbf{s}_{\mathrm{ref}}+\alpha\mathbf{s}_{\mathrm{end}}$ and receives its own feasibility and hard-metric audits. Duplicate slot provenance is retained.

Screening uses the existing float32 raster evaluator with one CPU thread after exact hard-metric parity of the reference at the original host thread count. Every screen-qualified point is fully re-evaluated without cache at the original thread count before ranking. A negative screening result would concern this finite screening grid; it would not certify continuous-segment or global infeasibility. Calibration remains closed by design even if a FIT point passes, and final3 remains untouched. All 5,140 slots completed in 514.449706 seconds, and all five groups contained qualifying points. The selected result was fully re-evaluated at the original 24-thread configuration. The report and source-vector identities are recorded in the accompanying result documentation; calibration remained closed and final3 remained untouched.

\subsection{Evidence Required for a Quality Claim}
A qualifying FIT point would establish only that the current source parameterization can meet the development gates. A quality claim additionally requires a frozen comparison on layouts not used to choose the source or revise the method, using matched masks, optical settings, dose conditions, and evaluation budgets. Both mean and per-layout PV-band, nominal fidelity, and worst-dose fidelity must be reported; five solver restarts on the same layouts do not provide independent data replication. Any hotspot-identification claim also requires an explicit hotspot definition, reference labels, and detection metrics. These evaluations have not been performed here.

\section{Conclusion}
We documented three completed fixed-mask, 49-weight optimizer experiments and a completed finite source-mixture diagnostic. The optimizer variants yielded no qualifying checkpoint, while the diagnostic found a two-pixel aggregate PV-band improvement on the three new FIT layouts with mean fidelity preserved. One layout's nominal error increased, and the best reduction was only 0.413\%. This establishes a qualifying point in the evaluated FIT grid and a need to track hard-metric incumbents; it does not establish a substantial quality gain, hotspot-detection improvement, generalization, or process-window robustness. Calibration remained closed and final3 untouched. A mixed-integer hard-PV diagnostic and joint SMO--Neural-ILT work remain unimplemented.
